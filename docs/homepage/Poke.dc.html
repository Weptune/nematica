POKE IT YOURSELF

Switch on one neuron. Watch the brain answer.

In this real worm (a held-out worm none of the models trained on), researchers switched neurons on one at a time with light (Randi et al. 2023). The same pulses were given to two artificial brains. Pick a neuron: each row below is a type of neuron, each column one second after the pulse; green rose, orange fell, brighter is stronger.

What to look for
In the real worm, a third of the neuron types stay dark and the ones that answer stay lit for a while. The brain learned from rest answers with a wide, flickering echo. The brain that passes the poke test has the right pattern and spread across many pokes, but its echoes are still more speckled than a real brain's: the one property no artificial brain matches yet. Each panel is a single trial, noisy like the real experiment; the verdicts on the findings page use thousands of pokes.
