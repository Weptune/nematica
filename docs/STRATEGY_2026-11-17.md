## Update after Study 4 (17 Nov 2026, evening)

- **Study 4 is stopped.** Its pre-freeze review (B-R1) found that both hierarchy claims would likely restate
  published results. It also found that under the fast flicker the measure can reflect response speed, and
  that the headline test could never fail. The user chose to stop and run a novelty check first; no
  Barcoding activity was measured.
- **The novelty check (shared doc, "Update after Study 4") found one strong candidate.** The observation
  model in brain-model alignment: does matching each animal's instrument change which models look most
  brain-like, by more than animals differ?
  - The literature: a 2026 scaling study across 8 datasets and 600 models asks how to translate model
    activity into neural signals (Gokce, Tang and Schrimpf, ICML 2026). The NeuroAI Turing test asks for
    differences within individual variability (Feather et al. 2025). Modality changes inferred response
    properties (Siegle et al., eLife 2021).
  - It builds on this project's strongest lesson.
- **Not new studies:**
  - perturbation realism (Sourmpis et al., eLife 2026 covers mice; our worm result belongs in preprint 1);
  - representational drift (Aitken et al. 2022);
  - tiring, which needs the atlas lab's data and the user's go-ahead to contact them.
- **Recommendation:** Study 5 on the observation model, as a methods question with a clear null; finish
  both preprint drafts alongside it.

## Novelty search for Study 5 (17 Nov 2026, 16:45 UTC)

The candidate was: does matching each animal's recording instrument change which models look most
brain-like, by more than animals differ? The exact-claim search found that its core is largely covered:
- Sagastuy-Brena, Thobani, Nayebi, Cao and Yamins (COSYNE 2023; CCN 2022) model inter-animal variability
  in mouse visual cortex. They find that a GLM with Poisson noise and a nonlinear link maps animals onto
  each other better than linear regression, and that a network with softplus units plus Poisson noise
  gives activations more like neural responses.
- Thobani et al. 2025 (arXiv 2510.02523) compare models with brains through the inter-animal transform
  class, in simulated networks, mice and humans.
- Bias from sampling neurons in alignment metrics (CKA) is treated by arXiv 2502.15104.

What remains ours is narrower: animal-specific instrument numbers (timescale, spikes per bin, white share,
rate spread) rather than generic Poisson noise, and the size of the instrument effect against
between-animal variation (1.8 between-mouse SDs in the map study). That fits preprint draft 2 as a
result, with this work cited, more than it justifies a new study. Recommendation: do not start Study 5
as framed; finish the preprints with a related-work section; the user decides.
