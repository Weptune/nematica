Brains with bodies.

The brain models tested on this site can also drive a simulated worm through a real worm's arena. These are demonstrations of what the models do, not evidence: every claim on this site rests on the tests in the findings.

A real worm and its model, in the same arena.

Left, a worm the model never saw, as recorded: its body, its path, and its brain. Right, a model worm in the same dish. Its brain senses copper only through the sensory neuron ASH, decides when to reverse, and steers its own body; only its body shapes are borrowed from real worms. Copper lines the edge of the dish and real worms avoid it.

Simulation 1. Each view follows its worm, about 2.6 mm across; the inset shows the whole dish and the path so far. The glow at the head is the brain's summed activity. Below each view, the last two minutes of activity of every neuron class recorded in both, the same classes in the same order, with reversals marked along the top. The model worms pass 9 of 10 activity tests and all three behaviour tests near copper, yet still spend more time near the copper edge than real worms do (13% against 3%).

Swarm

Thirty-two brains in one dish.

Every simulated worm here has its own brain, each fitted to a different real worm, sharing one copper arena. Heads that come within a body width of another worm can trigger a reversal, and no worm can crawl through another. Does a crowd of individuals behave differently from a crowd of identical average worms? With the interactions assumed so far, the difference is not established across random seeds.

Read with care

What these simulations are not.

  • They are not the benchmark. Each model was scored on held-out worms by the tests described in the method; these views only show the same models in motion.
  • Body shapes are borrowed from real worms; the models decide speed, reversals and steering.
  • The brains here are the copper-sensing linear models of the behaviour work, not the hybrid brain that passes the poke test.
  • Nothing here suggests that a model worm, or a real one, experiences anything.
