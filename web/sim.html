Swarm

Thirty-two worms in one dish.

Thirty-two worms now move in a live, seeded arena. Their speed, turning and reversal tendencies come from motion profiles of sixteen fitted individual brain models, two worms per model. Copper avoidance and contact responses are explicit behavioral assumptions. Pause, follow one worm, switch worm interactions off, or start a new seed to see how a crowd forms. This is a working sandbox for the next experiment, not a scored result against real swarms.

Interactions on adds a 0.3 chance of reversing on a touched forward step and 0.1 mm head exclusion; off is the pass-through baseline. Copper steering and a small containment limit apply in both modes. These rules have not been calibrated against real multi-worm tracking. The numbers above describe only the run on screen.

Read with care

What these simulations are not.

  • They are not the benchmark. The paired replay shows scored models in motion; the live swarm tests proposed crowd rules and has not been scored against real swarms.
  • The paired replay borrows body postures from real worms. The live swarm draws procedural bodies and uses fitted model motion profiles with explicitly assumed contact and copper rules.
  • The fitted profiles come from copper-sensing linear models of the behavior work, not the hybrid brain that passes the poke test.
  • Nothing here suggests that a model worm, or a real one, experiences anything.
