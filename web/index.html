What makes a real brain different from an artificial one?

Not by analogy. NEMATICA puts real nervous systems and artificial neural networks through the same tests, and judges both against one yardstick: how much real animals differ from each other. These are the results.

Worm, 113 animals

A copy of a brain can look perfect and still answer wrong.

We fitted artificial brains to real worms' ongoing activity, and they pass the tests of how a brain looks. Then we switched on one neuron at a time. Copies that had only watched answered almost at random. Copies that also learned from those pokes answered like a real worm.

Mouse cortex, 39 animals, tested as written in advance

Watching a brain cannot tell you which way a cell pushes it.

We built a copy of each mouse's brain from its resting activity, then used light to switch on one kind of inhibitory cell. At rest these cells rise and fall with everything else, so the copy expected switching them on to raise activity. For Sst cells the opposite happened, in all 29 mice. For Vip cells, which mostly silence other inhibitors, activity did rise, in all 10. The copy could not tell the two apart.

29 of 29mice where switching on Sst cells lowered most regions
13%of regions rose when Sst cells were switched on; the copy fitted at rest expected 77%

Worm, 27 freely moving animals

The best forecaster is the least individual.

Train a copy to predict a worm's next moment as well as possible and it drifts toward the average worm, closer to every real worm than real worms are to each other. Let each worm keep its own dynamics and the copy sits on the real-worm line, for a forecasting cost of under one percentage point.

0.99a copy that keeps each worm's own dynamics; real worms are 1

Mouse visual cortex, 35 animals

How you record a network can matter more than which mouse you compare it with.

A recording is a brain seen through an instrument. Recorded through five different mice's instruments, one artificial network moved by 1.8 times the spread between mice. So we record each network the way each mouse was recorded and compare mouse by mouse. A random network has less slow memory than the mouse in 33 of 35; given slow drives, more than all 31 measured. Cortex sits in between.

1.8×how far the recording instrument moved one network, in units of the spread between mice
33 of 35mice with more slow memory than a random network recorded like them

Mouse visual cortex, 49 animals

Artificial networks land on both sides of the brain.

Does cortex carry more internal state than artificial networks, activity not locked to what it sees? We tested it, written down in advance, against recurrent, gated, liquid, spiking, transformer and state-space networks, each recorded the way the mouse was. Some carry far less than cortex, some more, and the range of transformer sizes contains the median mouse. The brain is not at the end of the scale.

0 of 4claims that cortex carries more, slower internal state that held up

The answer so far

Six differences we measured between real and artificial networks, on one page. Green is the spread between real animals, an open mark a plain network, a filled mark the same network given what it was missing.

real animalsa plain artificial networkgiven what it was missing: green if it then lands among the animals

Four of the six differences closed, or were never there, once the network was given the right data, drive or training. So far, what separates these artificial networks from real ones is what they were shown and built to carry, not that they are artificial.

That is a reading of six results in two species, not a law. It rests on the tests we could run, and a pass on our ruler means "within about a quarter of a real animal", not "identical".

The mouse stimulation result points the same way: the copy that failed had only watched. What it lacked was the data that comes from poking, which is why it could not tell an Sst cell from a Vip cell.

The two open rows both need data that is not public: stimulation in worms a copy has never seen, or in worms that are moving. The evidence has every number and caveat.
